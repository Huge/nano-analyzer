Patch All the Software!
Every widely used open-source project, checked and fixed upstream.

The world runs on open-source code maintained by small, stretched teams. AI now makes finding bugs cheap; we want it to make fixing them cheap too. We scan open-source projects pro-bono, a person checks every lead, and the maintainer gets a ready fix, not another report to triage. Twenty-five projects so far. “All” is a long way off. That’s the point.

How a Patch Gets Made

The scanner is cheap and noisy. People and maintainers are not. The process is built around that.

01

Pick widely used code

We keep a ranked queue of libraries, frameworks and network daemons that many others depend on, and scan them whenever spare inference capacity is free.

02

Scan and triage

nano-analyzer with multi-round skeptical triage turns a codebase into a short list of leads. Even after triage, most leads are wrong.

03

A person checks every lead

We read the calling code, try to reproduce the bug and narrow its real reach. Most leads die here, and none of those reach a maintainer.

04

Fix, test, then send

A survivor goes upstream as a fix, with a regression test where the project has a test suite. Real vulnerabilities go privately, through the project’s security policy, before anything is public.

05

The maintainer decides

Maintainers often find a better fix than ours, as in Windmill and dnsmasq. We say so in the write-up.

Ground Rules

“All the software” only works if maintainers are glad to hear from us.

Maintainer time comes first

No dumps of scanner output. One checked problem and a patch, with a test where the project has them, written up so it can be reviewed in minutes.

Their rules, not ours

We follow each project’s security policy and disclosure process. Projects that do not accept AI-assisted contributions get none from us.

Honest severity

Our triage tends to overstate. Every story says how to read it, and most of our fixes are hardening, not remote exploits.

From 25 to All

What exists, what we plan, and what we are aiming at.

Now

Pro-bono scans and upstream fixes

Queue-driven scans of open-source projects, hand-checked leads, fixes and private reports sent upstream, and public case studies for selected fixes.

Now

Nominate a project

Maintainers can ask us to look at their project by email. Tell us your disclosure preferences and we will stick to them.

Planned

A public ledger

Every report we send and what became of it: merged, fixed differently, declined, or wrong. Including the misses.

Planned

Re-check on every release

Projects we have patched get a fresh scan of the changed code when they cut a new release, so a fix stays fixed.

Moonshot

Whole dependency trees

Not one project at a time, but everything under a typical web app or Linux base system: each widely used package gets a checked pass, every release.

Moonshot

Cheaper to check than to ignore

Bring the cost of a careful scan so low that skipping it is the expensive choice, for maintainers and for everyone who ships their code.
