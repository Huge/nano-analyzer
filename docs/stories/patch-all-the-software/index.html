Pro-bono initiative

Patch All the Software!

Every widely used open-source project, checked and fixed upstream.

The world runs on open-source code maintained by small, stretched teams. AI now makes finding bugs cheap; we want it to make fixing them cheap too. We scan open-source projects pro-bono, a person checks every lead, and the maintainer gets a ready fix, not another report to triage. Twenty-five projects so far. “All” is a long way off. That’s the point.

This is a goal, not a claim. Below we keep apart what we already do, what is planned, and what is still a moonshot. The numbers on this page come from the published case studies and the upstream pull requests and commits linked below.

How a Patch Gets Made #

The scanner is cheap and noisy. People and maintainers are not. The process is built around that.

  1. Pick widely used code. We keep a ranked queue of libraries, frameworks and network daemons that many others depend on, and scan them whenever spare inference capacity is free.
  2. Scan and triage. BudgetScan with multi-round skeptical triage turns a codebase into a short list of leads. Even after triage, most leads are wrong.
  3. A person checks every lead. We read the calling code, try to reproduce the bug and narrow its real reach. Most leads die here, and none of those reach a maintainer.
  4. Fix, test, then send. A survivor goes upstream as a fix, with a regression test where the project has a test suite. Real vulnerabilities go privately, through the project’s security policy, before anything is public.
  5. The maintainer decides. Maintainers often find a better fix than ours, as in Windmill and dnsmasq. We say so in the write-up.

Ground Rules #

“All the software” only works if maintainers are glad to hear from us.

  • Maintainer time comes first. No dumps of scanner output. One checked problem and a patch, with a test where the project has them, written up so it can be reviewed in minutes.
  • Their rules, not ours. We follow each project’s security policy and disclosure process. Projects that do not accept AI-assisted contributions get none from us.
  • Honest severity. Our triage tends to overstate. Every story says how to read it, and most of our fixes are hardening, not remote exploits.

From 25 to All #

What exists, what we plan, and what we are aiming at.

  • NowPro-bono scans and upstream fixes. Queue-driven scans of open-source projects, hand-checked leads, fixes and private reports sent upstream, and public case studies for selected fixes.
  • NowNominate a project. Maintainers can ask us to look at their project by email. Tell us your disclosure preferences and we will stick to them.
  • PlannedA public ledger. Every report we send and what became of it: merged, fixed differently, declined, or wrong. Including the misses.
  • PlannedRe-check on every release. Projects we have patched get a fresh scan of the changed code when they cut a new release, so a fix stays fixed.
  • MoonshotWhole dependency trees. Not one project at a time, but everything under a typical web app or Linux base system: each widely used package gets a checked pass, every release.
  • MoonshotCheaper to check than to ignore. Bring the cost of a careful scan so low that skipping it is the expensive choice, for maintainers and for everyone who ships their code.

Help Us Patch More #

The bottleneck is not finding bugs. It is people to check them and compute to find them.

  • Maintainers. Nominate your project, or tell us which parts worry you most. A short threat model makes the scan far more useful.
  • Compute and sponsors. The queue only moves when spare inference capacity is free. Idle GPUs or API credits turn directly into scanned projects.
  • Security engineers. Checking leads is where the work is. If you like reading unfamiliar code and proving a bug real or not, we would love the help.
